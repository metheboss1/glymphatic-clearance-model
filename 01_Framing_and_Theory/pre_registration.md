\## Hypothesis 1 — The Breakpoint

As heartbeat frequency or vessel wall stiffness increases, the Deborah number (De)

increases. We hypothesize a critical value, De\_c, exists at which the wall's internal

relaxation delay causes the Phase Lag (Φ) between wall motion and fluid flow to jump

SUDDENLY — not smoothly — by at least 0.15π radians.



\## Hypothesis 2 — The Destruction

When that phase lag jump occurs, the wall expands out of sync with the fluid flow,

creating localized backflow/recirculation zones. We hypothesize this produces an

ABRUPT drop of at least 15% in the clearance metric (Ψ), rather than a gradual decline.



\## Falsification criteria (what would prove this wrong)

\- If Phase Lag changes smoothly across the De sweep, with no detectable sudden jump

&#x20; (as determined by formal changepoint detection, not visual inspection) — H1 is false.

\- If the clearance metric declines by less than 15%, or declines gradually rather than

&#x20; abruptly — H2 is false.

\- Both outcomes will be reported with full statistical rigor regardless of which way

&#x20; the data falls. A rigorously falsified hypothesis is a valid, reportable result.
